# Privacy and security

[← Back to README](../README.md)

Where your files go, what other people learn about you, what stays on your computer, and how the plugin protects you from what others post. This page describes version 2.5.0.

## At a glance

- Files go only to your TeamSpeak server, with TeamSpeak's own file transfer.
- The plugin makes three kinds of web requests: update checks, to GitHub (automatic ones only after you agree), [link previews](#link-previews), and the [YouTube player](#playing-youtube-videos) (only after you press Play). For a link you send (a file, a video or GIF site, a well-known site or any other web page), your PC loads the preview once from that website and shares it through your TeamSpeak server; people with TS Media see it from the server and never contact the website. With **Load link previews automatically** on (the default), a link from someone without a sender-made preview is loaded from its website by your PC, which shows that website your IP address; turn the switch off to load such links only when you click **Load preview**. Link previews use your Windows proxy if you have one. Web pages are read only up to the end of their head (a Kick clip's page: its first 512 KB; a Nextcloud or ownCloud share page: until the part that says what the share is, at most 512 KB), never shown or run.
- People in your channel learn that you have TS Media chat and its version, and see your reactions. Both go through TeamSpeak and can be turned off. When someone there announces a newer version, only you see a hint, and nothing is sent unless you click it.
- A reply is an ordinary chat message that quotes the start of the original and names its author.
- Poll votes are anonymous ballots: nobody, not even the poll's author, sees who voted for what ([limits](#polls)).
- Live music goes out through your own TeamSpeak voice, like your microphone; the file itself is never uploaded.
- The microphone is used only while you hold the microphone button in the chat input, or while the *Voice message* window shows *Recording*.
- YouTube videos play with YouTube's own player (in a window of its own over TeamSpeak) only after you press Play: then your PC connects to YouTube and Google, as in a browser ([details](#playing-youtube-videos)).
- No telemetry, no accounts, no analytics.

## Where your files go

- **Files only go to your TeamSpeak server.** Uploads and downloads use TeamSpeak's own file transfer into the channel's file browser. There is no other server or cloud service, and video compression and picture editing run on your own computer.
- **Channel members can open what you send.** Anyone on the server with download permission for that channel can get the file. Treat sent files like any other file in the file browser: they stay on the server until someone deletes them.
- **The chat message is an ordinary TeamSpeak message.** It holds the file link with the file's size, picture size, duration, a blurred placeholder, the path of its preview, its SHA-256 and, where they apply, the spoiler, album and voice message marks. Everyone who can read the chat can read it, with or without the plugin.

## Replies and emoji

- **A reply quotes the original.** Its first line names the original's author with a TeamSpeak client link (the same kind TeamSpeak puts into the chat when you drag a client there, with their client ID and unique ID), the time and up to about 60 characters of the original message. It goes to the chat you reply in, like any message you type, and everyone in that chat can read it, with or without the plugin. Files sent as a reply carry the same line.
- **Replies are matched on your computer.** Finding the original only compares the text, the author and the time of messages already in your chat. Nothing is asked of the server or of other people, and a quote line is never fetched or followed.
- **HD emoji are drawn on your computer** with Windows' own emoji font; nothing is downloaded. Messages stay plain text with Unicode emoji, so people without the plugin see TeamSpeak's own emoji.
- **Reactions with any emoji** travel the same way as the six classic ones (see below), as emoji codes. The emoji picker's recently used emoji and skin tone stay on your computer (`emoji.ini`).

## What your channel sees

TS Media chat talks to other copies of itself with TeamSpeak's plugin commands. They go through your TeamSpeak server to the people in your channel, or to one private-chat partner, never to the whole server and never to the internet. Clients without TS Media chat ignore them.

- **Presence.** When you enter a channel, the plugin says hello to the people there, and to a private-chat partner when you send them something: *I have TS Media chat*, its version (for example `2.2.0`) and which features it supports (for example whether you make link previews, so their PCs wait for your preview instead of loading the website). That is how the send window can say *3 of 5 people here will see it in the chat*. When you turn it off, or the plugin unloads, it says goodbye. Turn it off with **Settings → Privacy & updates → Tell people in your channel that you have TS Media**.
- **Reactions.** Your reactions go to the people with TS Media who are online in the same channel or private chat: which emoji you picked on which media item (named by a hash of its link) or, from 2.3, on which message (named by a hash of its sender and text, and how long ago you received it). When you enter a channel, the plugin asks the people there for their reactions on the newest messages of your channel chat, named the same way (TeamSpeak's channel tab keeps the lines of earlier channels, so these hashes can include messages from the channel you came from; someone who knows a sender and guesses a short message could test it against them). Someone who comes in later asks the people present for their own reactions; nobody ever passes on someone else's. Reactions are not stored on the server. Turn them off with **Settings → Privacy & updates → Show reactions**: none are then shown or sent.
- **A newer version in your channel** (2.4.0). When someone in your channel says hello with a newer TS Media version than any your plugin knows of, your own chat shows one line: *Someone in this channel has TS Media chat X (you have Y).* with a **Check for updates** link. It names nobody, nobody else sees it, and the plugin's log notes only the version. Nothing is sent or requested until you click; the click is one manual update check to GitHub. Anyone can claim any version, so the link only ever checks the official latest release, at most 2 hints appear per session, and a version GitHub doesn't have is answered with *isn't released yet*. The hint doesn't depend on the presence setting: it only reads what others already send.
- **Who sent it** is always the identity the TeamSpeak server reports, never something written inside a message, so nobody can react or say hello in your name.
- What others see comes from their messages, checked the same way: reactions on channel media are accepted only from people in your channel, private ones only from the partner of that private chat, and nothing from ServerQuery clients.

## Polls

- **A poll is an ordinary chat message** (the question, the answers and a link), readable by everyone in the channel. Votes travel as TeamSpeak plugin commands to the people with TS Media in that channel. With presence turned off, your plugin sends nothing about polls except the votes you cast: it doesn't ask the channel for earlier votes and doesn't answer others who ask.
- **A vote is a ballot, not a person.** Your plugin makes a random secret per poll; the ballot only carries hashes of it, so nothing in it names you, and only you can change or retract it. TS Media checks that a command comes from someone in your channel, then drops who sent it; `polls.json` keeps the ballots and your own secret, never a name or an ID.
- **Honest limits:** the TeamSpeak server tells every receiver who sent each plugin command. TS Media ignores that, but a modified client could log it and link a live vote to its voter. A modified client can also invent ballots, so the counts are only as trustworthy as the people with the plugin in the channel. The details are in [polls](POLLS.md#honest-limits).

## Live music

- **It goes out through your own voice.** The music is mixed into what TeamSpeak sends from your microphone, so the channel hears it like your voice, with or without the plugin, at the quality of the channel's codec. The file is never uploaded, and nothing else leaves your computer.
- **Your microphone:** with voice activation your voice is mixed in whenever TeamSpeak would send it anyway. With push-to-talk the plugin keeps your input open while the music plays on the tab you are looking at and sends **only the music** meanwhile (it can't see your key, so the microphone is left out). While your microphone or sound is muted in TeamSpeak nothing is sent. Stopping the music, switching tabs or unloading the plugin gives TeamSpeak's input back as it was.
- **Remembered:** only the music volume, in `music.ini`. File names are not logged by the music code.

## Web requests

There are three kinds: the update check (below), [link previews](#link-previews), and the [YouTube player](#playing-youtube-videos), which connects only after you press Play on a YouTube card.

- **The update check**, only in official builds. About 45 seconds after the first start, the plugin asks *Keep TS Media chat up to date?*; nothing is sent before you click **Turn on**. Then it asks GitHub about once a day for a small file (`tsmedia-update-v2.json`), and downloads the plugin files after you click **Update**. When that file names a newer version, the daily check also asks for that release's small important-update marker; an [important update](UPDATES.md#important-updates) signed by the author is then downloaded and installed without a click (2.4.0 and later), and starts with your next TeamSpeak start. **Check now**, *Check for updates…*, `/tsmedia update` and the newer-version hint's link check once when you ask, with one request, and never install anything without your click.
- **What GitHub learns:** your IP address. Windows may also check GitHub's certificate with its issuer, and a proxy configured in Windows sees the connection. No names, servers, chats, files or identifiers are sent, the User-Agent carries no version, and there are no cookies. The plugin follows redirects only to GitHub's own hosts over HTTPS. Details: [updates](UPDATES.md#what-is-sent).
- **Nothing else** apart from [link previews](#link-previews) and the [YouTube player](#playing-youtube-videos). The link in the note for people without the plugin, *Get it from Microsoft Store* for video extensions and *Copy and open bug report* open your browser or the Store only when someone clicks them; the plugin itself sends nothing.
- Copies you build yourself contain no update check; they load Windows' web library (`winhttp.dll`) only for link previews.

## Link previews

Since 2.4, YouTube links and links to pictures, GIFs and videos get a preview in the chat; since 2.5 also links to GIF and media sites (the GIF or video itself), cards for well-known sites, and cards for other web pages (title, short text, picture), like Discord's. Who contacts the website depends on who made the preview:

- **What a link preview contacts.** A preview your PC loads itself contacts only what that kind of link needs: a file link: that file's address; YouTube: `www.youtube.com` (its oEmbed) and `i.ytimg.com` (the large thumbnail first, the standard one when a video has none); GIF and media sites (Tenor, Giphy, Imgur, Klipy, Reddit videos, Streamable and others): the site's media address, and for some of them first the page, its official oEmbed address or a short link (a Klipy GIF page that shows its bot check: then Klipy's public oEmbed at `api.klipy.com`, and the GIF it names only from Klipy's own `static…klipy.com` servers); well-known sites (Vimeo, SoundCloud, Spotify, Apple Music, Dailymotion, X, Bluesky, Twitch, Kick, Steam, GitHub, Wikipedia, Telegram, Discord invites, Reddit, Medal): the page or the site's official oEmbed or public API address, then the picture's address on that site's own servers; Nextcloud and ownCloud share links (`…/s/…`): the share page on that server, then the file or the server's own preview of a picture on the same server (see [Nextcloud and ownCloud shares](#nextcloud-and-owncloud-shares)); **other web pages** (with *Show cards for other web pages* on): the page itself, of which only the beginning is read (at most 512 KB, 128 KB with data saver, until its head ends; a Kick clip's page is read on past its head, within the same limit; it is never shown or run and never saved), then the address the page gives for its picture (up to two tries), which can be on any other website and is requested as the page wrote it. Nothing else: no scripts, styles, fonts, favicons or `robots.txt`; no cookies, no logins, no `Referer`, no `Accept-Language`, no compression. Every request says `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; TSMediaChat/2.5; +https://github.com/Metihttp/Teamspeak_Media_chat)`. Tracking parameters (`utm_…`, `fbclid`, `gclid`, Spotify's and YouTube's `si`, …) are left out of the request for the link's own page or API address; the link you open stays as written. Each website sees your IP address and when the preview loads (for a link without a sender-made preview: about when it scrolls into view), as when you open the link yourself.
- **How often.** A link is loaded once and then kept on your PC: a card for 7 days, "this page has no card" for a day (a bot check for 30 minutes, and so is a Klipy oEmbed answer that names no GIF: no Klipy GIF page asks it again meanwhile), media until the cache is cleared or trimmed. A few things ask again: a site that said it is busy is asked again after the time it named (at most twice, at most 10 minutes in all), a Twitch clip page that answered its empty shell is asked up to two more times, a card picture that failed is tried once more at the next address the page names, and a click (**Retry**, **Load link previews**) always asks again.
- **Who loads it.** When the sender's TS Media makes the preview, everyone with TS Media 2.4 or later uses that one (for pages and sites: 2.5 or later) and doesn't contact the website. Everyone else's PC loads it from the website when it scrolls into view (or on a click), in these cases: the sender has no TS Media (or an older version for that kind of link), has *Make previews for links I send* or *Show cards for other web pages* off, sent more than 6 links in a minute, or their plugin couldn't make or share it (the website failed or was too large for them, the upload or the plugin command failed); a link in the server chat; a click. When the sender's plugin found that a page has nothing to show or redirects to another website, it says so and nobody loads it by themselves (a click still can); a page that showed the sender a bot check or a consent page may still load for you (those often depend on where the sender is). A page that redirects to another website never shows a card by itself: only after a click on **Load link previews** (or for your own message), and that card names the link's own site first and the site it went to after it, without that site's name or colour.
- **Links you send** (*Make previews for links I send*, on by default). When you send a chat message with such a link in a channel or private chat, your PC loads it **once** from that website (the server chat can't carry the announcement, so links there are treated like other links): for YouTube its title, channel and thumbnail (from `www.youtube.com` and `i.ytimg.com`), for a page its title, text and picture (and for a media site the GIF or video), otherwise the file. That website sees your IP address, as when you open the link yourself. Your own chat shows the preview from that download at once, before it is uploaded; the downloaded file stays in `cache\links`. Your plugin then uploads the preview (a thumbnail, a smaller picture, a still or a video's poster, and the file itself when it is small) to the channel's file browser next to your other TS Media files, and tells the people with TS Media in that chat about it with one plugin command. A card's title, text, site name and colour go into that one plugin command; its picture goes into the channel's files like other previews, so the card shares the page's title, text and picture with everyone who can read the message (a link to a page only your network can reach shares that page's text too: don't send such links where they shouldn't be read). A page that redirects to another website gets no card from you, and your plugin tells the others so: nobody loads it by themselves (a click on **Load link previews** still can, and shows where it went). Without upload permission no file goes up: a card is shared without its picture, media isn't shared, and your plugin doesn't try to upload into that channel again for 10 minutes; when your plugin can't make the preview it tells the people with TS Media in that chat with one small plugin command, so they don't wait for it; a channel message's preview goes only into that channel (if you have moved to another channel meanwhile, nothing is uploaded). The preview stays on the server like any file you send. Your plugin also tells people with TS Media in your channel that you make link previews (with *Tell people in your channel that you have TS Media*), so their PCs wait for your preview instead of loading the website.
- **Previews made by the sender** are downloaded from the **TeamSpeak server**, never from the website, so your IP address is never shown to that website. A preview is only taken from the person who sent that very message (the identity the TeamSpeak server reports, never ServerQuery), and a downloaded preview that doesn't match its announced SHA-256 is not shown. The preview picture (at most 2 MB) always loads while pictures are shown in the chat; the full file follows your automatic download rules and data saver like other media. Previews are remembered in `link-previews.json` (with the cached files) so they show again when the chat history reloads. This stays so with automatic loading on: while the sender's preview is on its way, the card says *Waiting for the sender's preview…* (about 30 seconds for YouTube, 40 for cards, 45 for pictures and GIFs, 60 for GIF and media sites and 2 minutes for videos once the sender's plugin gets to it; it makes one preview at a time, so a link posted right after others waits for those too, up to 4 minutes in all). While your PC is still asking whether the sender makes previews (a few seconds), the card says *Checking for the sender's preview…*.
- **Other links** (from people without TS Media 2.4, or 2.5 for pages and sites, or with *Make previews for links I send* off, links in the server chat, and links whose sender's preview doesn't come): **with *Load link previews automatically* on (the default), your PC loads the preview from the website by itself, which shows that website your IP address**, as when you open the link yourself. It happens only for links on your screen (or close to it), at most 2 at a time and one of them per person, and at most 6 at once from one person, then one every 10 seconds; a link waiting its turn shows **Load preview** until then. YouTube's and other cards (title, text and a picture of at most 2 MB) always load, also with data saver (then reading at most 128 KB of a page and a picture of at most 512 KB); pictures, GIFs (also from GIF sites, and a page that turns out to be a picture or video; a sender's GIF-like looping video too, when it says it is at most 60 seconds and 8 MB, else it counts as a video) and videos only within your limit for automatic downloads (15 MB by default; videos use your limit for videos instead when you set one), never with data saver. Cards for other web pages appear when they are ready (nothing is shown while they load, and nothing when a page has no usable title and text or picture, or is a sign-in, consent or bot-check page); **Load link previews** in a message's menu loads them by hand. Requests to one site are spaced out (one page request at a time, about one a second), and a site that says it is busy (HTTP 429, or that its request allowance is used up) is left alone for as long as it asks (1 second to 10 minutes); its cards say *Site busy* and load by themselves afterwards (for at most 10 minutes in all). A larger file is not downloaded: its card shows its size (or that it is over your limit, when the website doesn't say) and **Load preview**, or *Too large to preview here* when even a click couldn't load it. A link whose sender makes previews is loaded this way only when the sender's plugin says it can't make one, the sender leaves, the wait above runs out, or in the cases under *Honest limits*. Links already loaded before show from `cache\links` without any request. Turn **Load link previews automatically** off to load such links only when you click **Load preview** (the card's tool tip says that loading connects your PC to that website). A click loads it once (a picture or GIF up to 25 MB, a video up to 50 MB; larger ones say *Too large to preview here* and offer **Open in browser**). When the sender's preview arrives before the link was loaded here, the card switches to it; a link already loaded keeps that copy until TeamSpeak restarts.
- **Honest limits:** the wait for a sender's preview needs presence on both sides. With *Tell people in your channel that you have TS Media* off on your side or the sender's, your PC can't know that the sender makes previews and loads the link at once; the sender's preview may then arrive second. A link the sender's plugin skipped because they sent more than 6 links in a minute is loaded as soon as their plugin says so (TS Media 2.5, at most one such notice every 10 seconds), or at once when your PC saw them send 6 links in the last minute itself; otherwise when its wait runs out. A newer TS Media may know sites an older one treats as ordinary pages; the waits keep that short. Card pictures of a site are requested as the page names them: a page could name a picture on another site to learn that you looked (like any picture in an e-mail); addresses that look like sign-in, unsubscribe or token links are never requested, also not when a page or picture address redirects to one. A Reddit post's own picture or video is loaded only from Reddit's servers or from a GIF and media site's (Imgur, Giphy, Tenor, ...), never from a server the post's author picked; other posts show their card. Pages that declare themselves for adults (their rating, or an age of 18 or more) get the spoiler cover over their card's picture or their media; a page without that flag may still be for adults. With TeamSpeak's chat timestamps off, a message can't be matched to its sender's preview at all: its links are treated like other links and load from their websites. After **Clear cache**, a link whose sender's preview it removed shows **Load preview** instead of loading from its website, until TeamSpeak restarts; after that it is treated like other links.
- **YouTube videos play only after you press Play**, with YouTube's own player in a window of its own (Microsoft Edge WebView2, see [Playing YouTube videos](#playing-youtube-videos)); with *Play YouTube videos in the chat* off, Play opens the video in your browser. The card's title and picture are a link preview like the others.
- **How the plugin talks to websites:** https only: an `http://` link (as TeamSpeak links an address typed without one) is loaded from its https address, never over http; no cookies, no logins and no `Referer`, the User-Agent above, at most 5 redirects (each one https and checked again), addresses of your own network (private, loopback, link-local and the like, also behind a redirect) refused, time limits, and size limits while it downloads. Pictures are checked by their content and decoded with the same limits as any chat file. Web pages are read by the plugin's own small reader, never by a browser engine: only the meta tags and the title count, entities are decoded once, and every address in a page is checked like a link (https, no address of your own network, at most 5 checked redirects); SVG pictures are never decoded; lossless WebP pictures are refused (an image library bug in TeamSpeak's Qt); through a proxy, names under your own network's DNS suffixes are refused. Titles and channel names lose control and bidirectional-text characters before they are drawn, and they are always shown as plain text (also in tool tips), never as formatting that could load anything. The plugin's log names websites only by their host and the file's name, never a full address; diagnostic info leaves them out.
- **Your Windows proxy.** If Windows is set to use a proxy (**Settings → Network & internet → Proxy**: a proxy server, a setup script or automatic detection), previews go through it, like a browser's requests; this is how they work behind a proxy-based VPN. A setup script (also one found by automatic detection) is only told the website's name, never the rest of the address. The proxy then sees which websites are contacted, and it looks up their addresses itself, so the plugin can't check that they aren't on your own network (it still refuses links that name such an address, and every other rule above stays). Addresses your proxy settings exclude, and every request when no proxy is set, go directly with all the checks.
- **When it doesn't work**, the card says *Couldn't load the preview* (whatever the reason: no connection, a refused address, a time-out, a certificate problem) and offers **Retry**; the reason is only in the plugin's log. Only a few things are said on the card: a YouTube video that isn't available, a file that is gone from the website, a link that isn't a picture or video, a picture too large to show, *The site is busy right now. Try again later.*, *This picture can't be shown here*, and after **Load link previews** found nothing *No preview for this page*. An automatic load that fails is not tried again by itself, except at a busy site, which is asked again by itself after the time it named (see above). Any link whose page is a bot check, a consent or sign-in page or has nothing usable gets no card at all (no box); a card for another web page that fails shows nothing either (**Load link previews** tries it again), while a well-known site or a GIF or media site whose request fails shows *Couldn't load the preview* with **Retry**.
- **Never previewed:** links in TS Media's own lines and the plugin's own links (the note for people without the plugin, update hints), `ts3file` links (those are files sent in the chat), links that look like sign-in, confirmation, unsubscribe or download links or carry a token or code, links to documents and archives (`.pdf`, `.zip`, …), TikTok, IMDb, Amazon and Google Maps links (nothing honest works there, or the map would show where you are), Deezer links (its servers refuse its pictures to every app), Gfycat (gone) and Redgifs links, private Telegram links, Reddit share links, Twitch VODs, and more than 3 links per message.
- **Turning it off:** **Settings → Privacy & updates → Show previews for links to other websites** off removes every card and preview, cancels website loads that are running and starts no new link-preview download (one from the TeamSpeak server that is already running may still finish); **Load link previews automatically** off loads other people's links only when you click **Load preview** or **Load link previews** (and stops automatic loads that are running; your own links and previews made by their senders still come); **Make previews for links I send** off stops your plugin from loading and sharing previews of your links. **Show cards for other web pages** off: links to pages that aren't videos, GIFs, pictures or well-known sites get no preview and are never loaded, also not for your own messages (except links shaped like a Nextcloud or ownCloud share, `…/s/…`, which are still loaded to learn whether they hold a picture, GIF or video; those that turn out not to be media then get no card). *Hide preview* (right-click) hides one card until TeamSpeak restarts.

### Nextcloud and ownCloud shares

For a link shaped like a Nextcloud or ownCloud public share (`…/s/…`), TS Media reads the share page from that server to check that it is a share and to learn the file's name, type and size. It then loads the file, or the server's own preview of a picture, from the same server, never from an address the page names. For a link to a share page, password-protected shares, upload-only shares, shares whose owner turned off downloads and shares with a download limit are never downloaded (a limit would be used up by every viewer); a link straight to the file (`…/s/…/download/<name>`, `…/public.php/dav/…`, a preview address) loads like any other file link and counts as a download. Like any visitor's, a load of a GIF or video counts as a download in the owner's activity list; pictures load through previews, which don't. Servers on a LAN, VPN or private address are never contacted, so their links get no preview. Share pages are read even while *Show cards for other web pages* is off, because that is the only way to learn whether a share holds a picture, GIF or video; with it off, shares that turn out not to be media get no card, and a link that turns out not to be a share (a redirect to another server, a page that isn't a share) is loaded no further: neither that other page nor a picture it names.

- **What is read:** the share page, at most 512 KB (128 KB with data saver), and only until the part that says what the share is has been read; it is never shown or run and never saved. A share is recognised only by what the page itself says (the share's token and its own address), never by the server's name.
- **What is loaded:** a picture through the server's preview (at most 2048 pixels; a JPEG, PNG or WebP picture whose previews fail: the file itself, within your limit), a GIF or video through the share's download address (within your limit for pictures or for videos; a larger one shows its size with **Load preview**, and over what a click may load *Too large to preview here* with **Open in browser**, without any download). A card's picture is the server's preview of the file, and only when the page itself shows one; a folder's card has none. A redirect to another server ends this: the link is then treated like any other web page (not loaded at all while *Show cards for other web pages* is off). A page that isn't proven a share gets the ordinary web card rules, but never a share's file or preview as its picture (on any web page: such a read could use up a limited share's download).

## Playing YouTube videos

When you press Play on a YouTube card, TS Media shows YouTube's own embedded player (`www.youtube-nocookie.com`, YouTube's privacy-enhanced mode). In 2.5.0 it opens in a window of its own over TeamSpeak. The web engine runs in Microsoft Edge WebView2, the web view built into Windows, in WebView2's own processes; a small WebView2 client library and TS Media's player helper are loaded into TeamSpeak. The player loads nothing before you press Play. The card's title and picture come from YouTube as described under [Link previews](#link-previews), unless the sender's plugin made the preview.

From the moment you press Play your PC connects to YouTube and Google. The live test of 2.5.0 recorded `www.youtube-nocookie.com` (the player), `www.youtube.com` (its player script), `i.ytimg.com` and `yt3.ggpht.com` (pictures), `*.googlevideo.com` (the video), `www.google.com`, `jnn-pa.googleapis.com`, `fonts.gstatic.com` and `www.gstatic.com`; YouTube chooses its servers, so this can change. They see your IP address (through your Windows proxy if you use one), the video and its start time, the browser engine, your language, the player's size and how you use it, and TS Media's app ID (`io.github.metihttp.tsmediachat`) as the embedding app. Ads may play. WebView2 itself also contacts Microsoft (recorded: `edge.microsoft.com` and `config.edge.skype.com`, for its component and configuration updates; no SmartScreen address), and its crash reports follow Windows' diagnostic settings, as for Edge. At each Play WebView2 also opens a connection for the name of TS Media's own page, `io.github.metihttp.tsmediachat`, although the page comes from memory and nothing is ever sent there: the live test saw that name go to the Windows proxy (without a proxy, WebView2 may look it up in DNS). The name exists nowhere on the internet. Nothing of the player connects before you press Play, and nothing once its window has closed (WebView2's processes end within seconds).

- The player runs in a private (InPrivate) profile: cookies live only in memory and are gone when the player closes, and no browsing history or page cache is kept.
- WebView2 still keeps its own data in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\TSMedia\webview2` (its `EBWebView` profile folder with component data, and crash dumps, which can contain the addresses of the pages and video ids); a TeamSpeak running as administrator uses `webview2-elevated` instead, so the two never share one folder. In the live test it held about 70 MB after several videos, all in `EBWebView`: WebView2's component data (`WidevineCdm`, `component_crx_cache`, `Subresource Filter`, `Speech Recognition`, `hyphen-data` and smaller ones), GPU shader caches (`GrShaderCache`, `ShaderCache`, `GPUPersistentCache`), `Crashpad` (no dumps) and the profile folders `Default` and `WV2Profile_tsmedia-player`, whose files held no page address or video id. Delete that folder to remove it (close TeamSpeak first, or wait a minute after the last video). Turning *Play YouTube videos in the chat* off deletes both folders (once their processes have exited, or at the next start; a folder another TeamSpeak's player still uses is left whole until then).
- TS Media turns off WebView2's SmartScreen check for the player (it would send the page addresses to Microsoft). Only TS Media's own page (served from memory, never from the internet) and YouTube's player load in it; any other link opens in your browser.
- Privacy-enhanced mode is YouTube's; YouTube describes it for websites, and an app's web view is not exactly that. YouTube doesn't let apps look up whether a video is "made for kids" without an API key; TS Media uses no API key and has no tracking of its own.
- Others in the chat learn nothing from your playing. YouTube's [Terms of Service](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms) and the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) apply.
- Turn it off with *Play YouTube videos in the chat* (*Settings → Privacy & updates → Link previews*): Play then opens the video in your browser.

## What stays on your computer

Everything below is in `%APPDATA%\TS3Client\plugins\tsmedia` (a portable TeamSpeak uses the `config` folder inside the TeamSpeak folder instead of `%APPDATA%\TS3Client`).

- **Local cache** (`cache`): downloaded media, limited by the cache size you choose. You can clear it at any time in the settings.
- **Temporary copies:** files being sent are staged in the `upload` and `paste` folders. Pictures edited in the send window are written to `edit`, and voice recordings to `voice`. They are deleted once they have been sent or dismissed, and at the latest at the next start (voice recordings after a day).
- **Dragged and copied files** (`export`): dragging a file out of the chat or using *Copy file* makes a copy with a clean name (a hard link where possible). These copies are removed after 24 hours, even if the cache limit removed the cached file earlier.
- **Polls** (`polls.json`): the ballots of polls your chats showed (hashes and chosen answers, no names or IDs), your own secret per poll, at most 300 polls. **Live music** (`music.ini`): the volume.
- **Link previews** (2.4): the senders' previews you have seen in `link-previews.json` (at most 2000 links, 90 days without use: the message's hash and time, the link's hash, the files' paths on the TeamSpeak server and their SHA-256, a YouTube title and channel, a card's title, text, site name and colour, the sender's unique ID), and files and card pictures loaded from websites (automatically, with **Load preview**, and the links you send), and what was found on a page (title, text, site name; kept 7 days, that a page had nothing for 24 hours, that it was behind a bot check for 30 minutes) in `cache\links` (at most about 200 MB, the oldest go first). **Clear cache** deletes both. The previews of your own links are made in `linkpreview\send` and deleted once they are uploaded.
- **Saved reactions** (`reactions.json`): the reactions you have seen, with the TeamSpeak unique ID and nickname of each person who reacted, for at most 30 days and 500 media items. **Clear cache** deletes them. Reactions on text messages (2.3) are kept the same way in `text-reactions.json` (at most 1000 messages), with a 16-digit hash of each message instead of its text.
- **The plugin's log** (`logs\tsmedia.log`, up to 256 KB, plus one older copy `tsmedia.1.log`): the same lines TeamSpeak's own log gets from the plugin, so it contains the names of files you send and receive, local paths, and server, channel and nicknames where a line mentions them. Those values are marked in the file, so the diagnostic info can leave them out. The log never leaves your computer by itself.
- **Diagnostic info** (`/tsmedia diag`) is built only when you open it and leaves your computer only if you paste it somewhere. It shows exactly what *Copy* copies: plugin, Qt, TeamSpeak and Windows versions, which video and audio codecs Windows has, your settings (custom folders and links only as "changed"), counts of this session's transfers, file checks, presence and reactions, and the recent TS Media lines of the log. File names and paths in those lines read `<file 1>`, `<path 1>` unless you tick *Include file names* (your user folder then shows as `%USERPROFILE%`). Server addresses, server and channel names, unique IDs and nicknames are never included. *Copy and open bug report* opens GitHub's form in your browser with only the version fields filled in.
- **Settings** (`settings.ini`), including the servers you gave their own settings, by their ID and name, and the emoji picker's recently used emoji, skin tone and the page it showed last (`emoji.ini`).
- **Your GIFs** (2.5, `gifs`): see [below](#your-gifs).
- **YouTube player** (2.5): the player helper `player\tsmedia_player_win64_<hash>.dll` (written at the first Play; an older version's goes at the next start), and, outside this folder, WebView2's data in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\TSMedia\webview2` (`webview2-elevated` for a TeamSpeak running as administrator; [details](#playing-youtube-videos)).

### Your GIFs

TS Media keeps the links of GIFs you send from public GIF sites (Giphy, Tenor, Klipy, Imgur, Imgflip, Gifer, Makeagif) and links to `.gif` files without a query, copies of GIF files you upload (up to 25 MB each, 256 MB in all, 100 GIFs), and whatever you save with *Save to GIFs*, plus small pictures of them, in `plugins\tsmedia\gifs` on your PC so you can send them again from the emoji picker's GIFs tab. Links that can carry a private share token (Dropbox, Discord, Nextcloud and ownCloud shares, links with a query) are never kept automatically; *Save to GIFs* keeps one only when you choose it. Nothing of it is sent anywhere, and opening the GIFs tab contacts no website. *Clear cache* doesn't remove it: use *Remove all GIFs…* in the GIFs tab or in Settings → General → Emoji, and turn off *Remember GIFs I send* to stop it. A GIF file sent again from the tab is uploaded to your channel's file browser again, like any file you send.

## The microphone (voice messages)

- **Only while you record.** The plugin opens the microphone only after you start a voice message, and only while you hold the microphone button in the chat input (a strip above the input shows the recording) or while the *Voice message* window shows *Recording*. If you let go, close that window, or TeamSpeak unloads the plugin, recording stops at once. Nothing is recorded in the background, and nothing records without the strip or the window showing.
- **Nothing leaves your computer until you let go of the microphone button or press Send** (or the recording reaches 5 minutes, where it is sent by itself and the strip or the window says so; if you were about to cancel a held one, it is kept in the window instead). Letting go away from the button, or <kbd>Esc</kbd>, discards it; when TeamSpeak never saw you let go, or another chat came up while you held the button, the recording is kept in the window and only sent if you press Send. The sound is kept in memory, written to `%APPDATA%\TS3Client\plugins\tsmedia\voice` when you stop, and sent through TeamSpeak's file transfer like any file. Discarded recordings are deleted right away; sent ones are deleted with their upload (a failed one stays for Retry until you dismiss it), and anything left over at the next start.
- **Your TeamSpeak microphone is muted while you record**, and others can see that. It is unmuted again when recording stops; it is never unmuted where you muted it yourself. If something goes wrong (TeamSpeak crashes, the connection drops), it stays muted rather than open; after a dropped connection it is unmuted once TeamSpeak is connected there again.
- The recording is the raw microphone: TeamSpeak's noise suppression and echo cancellation don't apply to it.

## Photo and video metadata

- **Edited pictures** (crop, draw, hide details in the send window) are sent as a new file without any metadata: no EXIF, so no camera details and no location.
- **Pictures you send without editing are sent exactly as they are**, so a phone photo can still contain where it was taken.
- **Compressed videos** keep no metadata (including the location some phones store), extra sound tracks or subtitles. Videos sent as *Original* are unchanged.
- **Hiding details:** *Black box* covers an area with solid black. *Pixelate* replaces it with coarse blocks (with a little fixed noise so the text can't be guessed back), but large text can stay partly readable; use *Black box* for passwords, codes and addresses.

## Nothing is sent by accident

- **Pasting always opens the send window first.** The clipboard may hold something old you forgot about.
- **Dropping opens the send window too**, unless you chose *Send right away*. Hold Shift while dropping to get TeamSpeak's normal behaviour, or turn drop sending off in the settings.
- **Private chats stay private.** If the partner of a private chat can't be identified, nothing is sent.
- **Files dragged out of the chat are never uploaded again** when they are dropped back on a chat or the send window.

## Received files are checked and never run

- **SHA-256 checks.** A file sent with TS Media chat 2.2 carries the SHA-256 of its exact bytes in the link, and its preview the start of its own. After a download the plugin hashes the file, off TeamSpeak's user interface thread, and compares. A file that doesn't match (replaced or damaged on the server) is deleted and never shown or saved: *File doesn't match what was sent*. There is no "open anyway". A link can't borrow a genuine file's cache entry with a made-up checksum, because the checksum is part of the entry's key.
- **Programs are never run.** `.exe`, `.bat`, `.cmd`, `.msi`, `.ps1`, `.vbs`, `.js`, `.lnk`, `.jar`, `.dll`, `.reg`, `.scr`, `.ts3_plugin` and the other types Windows runs on double-click are only shown in Explorer. Trailing-dot and trailing-space tricks (such as `file.exe.`) are caught too.
- **Dragged and copied files carry Windows' "downloaded from the internet" mark** (Mark-of-the-Web), like a browser download: SmartScreen warns before a program runs, and Office opens documents in Protected View. The cached file is marked too.

## Chat links are untrusted input

Anyone can type a TeamSpeak file link with fake metadata, so the plugin checks everything a link claims:

- Dimensions are clamped to 16384 px and to an aspect ratio of at most 8:1.
- Invalid BlurHash placeholders, and ones longer than 120 characters, are ignored.
- Preview paths must be absolute, in the same channel and without `..`. Preview downloads above 5 MB are aborted, and a preview that doesn't match its checksum is not shown.
- The 2.2 fields (checksum, spoiler, album, voice message, waveform) are accepted only in their exact format; an invalid value drops only that field, never the link.
- A file link inside a caption, escaped or broken by invisible characters, is never taken for a real link, so a caption can't make the plugin download something.
- Received pictures are decoded only as PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP or BMP, by their content and never as anything else, and lossless WebP pictures are refused (2.5).
- Images above 80 megapixels are never decoded. They show *Can't preview this image* and can still be opened in your default app.
- Expensive decodes and checksums run off TeamSpeak's user interface thread.
- File names are sanitised before they touch the disk, for the cache, *Save as* and dragged-out copies alike.
- Control and bidirectional-text characters are removed from displayed names and captions, so a name can't disguise its real extension.
- A download whose size doesn't match the link is discarded.
- Plugin commands from other clients are size- and rate-limited and checked field by field before anything is stored. Reaction emoji must be emoji of the plugin's own table, at most 10 per person and item.
- A reply's quote line counts only when it has exactly the expected form; one with any other link in it is shown as ordinary text. Names and snippets read from the chat lose control and bidirectional-text characters before they are drawn.
- At most 400 emoji per message become HD pictures, and the chat is worked through a few milliseconds at a time, so a message full of emoji can't freeze TeamSpeak.
- Web links (2.4, pages since 2.5) are previewed by the rules in [link previews](#link-previews); with **Load link previews automatically** on, a web link someone posts makes your PC contact that website when it shows on your screen (within the limits above; for a link whose sender shares a preview only when that preview doesn't come or in the cases under *Honest limits* in [link previews](#link-previews)); with it off, only your click on **Load preview** or your own sent message does. A sender's preview names its files with the same checks as file links (absolute paths without `..`, a SHA-256 for each file), and is accepted only from the sender of that message. A card's text from a sender's plugin is checked like a message's text (control, bidi and invisible characters removed, lengths capped) and is never taken as an address: the card always opens the link as written, and shows the host your PC reads from it.

## Automatic downloads are limited

By default, only images, GIFs and voice messages up to 15 MB download automatically (voice messages at most 16 MB), plus small previews. Videos and audio files are only downloaded when you press play, and spoilers only after you reveal them. Data saver pauses automatic downloads for all servers or for one. The limits can be changed in the [settings](SETTINGS.md#receiving). Link previews that load from websites by themselves follow the same limits (YouTube's title and thumbnail always load; a linked video uses the image limit while videos are set to download when played, because a website's video has no poster without the file; with data saver nothing else loads). Cards (title, text and a small picture) load like YouTube's, also with data saver. A sender's GIF-like looping video downloads like a GIF (by the image limit) only when it says it is at most 60 seconds and 8 MB; anything else counts as a video.

## Updates are checked

Updates come only from the latest release of the official repository, [Metihttp/Teamspeak_Media_chat](https://github.com/Metihttp/Teamspeak_Media_chat/releases), over HTTPS; the plugin follows redirects only to GitHub hosts and builds every download address itself. Each downloaded file must match the size and SHA-256 listed in that release, be built for your CPU, and load in your TeamSpeak, or nothing is changed. Only newer versions are offered, and a version that fails to start is rolled back by itself. Nothing is installed before you click **Update**, except an important update with automatic checks on (2.4.0 and later).

Since 2.2.1, an update you click doesn't depend on a separate signing key: the trust is the official GitHub repository itself and HTTPS, so whoever controls the repository (or a program on your PC that intercepts HTTPS with its own root certificate) can offer an update; you still decide whether to click **Update**. An important update that installs without a click needs more: a marker for exactly that release, signed with the author's release key (ECDSA P-256, checked through Windows CNG), which is never on GitHub. The plugin then installs only files that match the signed SHA-256, never restarts TeamSpeak for it, never installs a version without a click again once it rolled back on your PC or you replaced it by hand, and offers the release the normal way when anything doesn't fit (the marker expires at most 60 days after the release). Turning automatic checks off turns this off too. The signed manifest that 2.2.0 reads is still checked against its signature. See [updates](UPDATES.md#trust).

## The DLL is not code-signed

The plugin DLL is not signed with a code-signing certificate, so some antivirus products may flag it. If you prefer, [build it yourself](BUILDING.md) from this source code; self-built copies have no update check.
